**SECTION 4: THE GAMES**

4.1 **The Games.** Seedbank currently offers six games. None of them are designed to be especially addictive, and if you find yourself checking the app more often than seems reasonable, that's a personal problem and not a feature, whichever one of us it happens to. The following base win rates are provided as documentation for each game, calculated from the stated rules of each game in isolation:

- **Coinflip.** You pick heads or tails. A win is recorded where your pick matches the determined outcome. The documented base win rate is 50%, reflecting two equally weighted choices.

- **Color Cube.** You pick one of six colors, corresponding to the six faces of two dice. A win is recorded where at least one of the two dice, once rolled, shows your selected color. The documented base win rate is approximately 30.6%.

- **Cards.** Three cards are presented face down. The first card is, by design, always safe. The remaining two are independently and randomly designated safe or unsafe. A win is recorded where your selected card is safe. The documented base win rate is approximately 67%.

- **Mines.** A 25-tile grid is presented, of which you select four. The number of bombs placed for a given round is randomly set to six, seven, or eight. A win is recorded where none of your four selected tiles are a bomb. The documented base win rate is approximately 49.1%.

- **Slots.** Three reels each independently resolve to one of three symbols. You designate one symbol before spinning. A win is recorded where at least two of the three reels resolve to your symbol. The documented base win rate is approximately 25.93%.

- **Roulette.** A wheel is divided into green segments, red segments, and a single yellow segment. A win is recorded where the wheel lands on green. As for the yellow, we do not know who put that in there. The documented base win rate varies according to the green-to-red ratio configured for a given session.

4.2 **Conclusion.** The rates above describe what each game's stated rules would produce in isolation, and are presented to you as a matter of record. Every game ultimately resolves through the same backend logic before its animation plays out on your screen, and what that logic actually does in practice is a separate question from what the rules above say it should do. The repository is public, for anyone curious enough to go looking instead of taking the numbers above at face value. By using the wagering features of the Platform, you accept the outcome of each round as final, regardless of how it compares to anything written above.
